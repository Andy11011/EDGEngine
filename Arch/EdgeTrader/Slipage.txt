Yes, limit orders (and advanced combo orders like OCO and Stop-Limit) are exactly how institutional and retail traders fight price slippage in volatile markets—but they fight it in slightly different ways.


1. The Core Weapon Against Slippage: The Limit Order


Slippage happens when you use a Market Order (or a Stop-Loss that converts to a Market Order). The market fills you at whatever the current best available price is. In a volatile second, that price can be several ticks (or even dollars) worse than expected.

Market Order = Guaranteed fill, unguaranteed price (slippage).

Limit Order = Guaranteed price (no slippage!), unguaranteed fill.

So, to strictly avoid price slippage, you must use limit orders. You say, *"Fill me only at $100 or better, never worse."* 
If the market jumps from $100 to $102 instantly, your limit order simply won't execute—you miss the trade entirely, but 
you saved yourself from buying at $102.


The Brutal Reality (The Trade-off)
In highly volatile markets (crypto, NFP news, thin liquidity), you cannot have zero slippage and guaranteed fills at the same time.

If you use Limit / Stop-Limit (to fight slippage): The price can "gap" right over your limit price without triggering it. You survive without slippage, 
but you get skipped (your protective order never fills), and you lose even more as the price moves against you.

If you use Market / Stop-Market (to guarantee the exit): You guarantee you get out, but you absorb the slippage.

Professional high-frequency traders (HFTs) actually embrace slippage if they can model it statistically—they use "market-if-touched" orders and actively adjust limit prices based on real-time order book depth.
